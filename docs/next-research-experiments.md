Finite examples alone cannot establish equivalence.

## 2. Extract one lemma with an actual consumer

**Artifacts.** Start with `Claim.factor_ne_zero` and its use inside `Claim.prim_zero` in the pinned ClaimStrip file. That consumer uses `LFunction_changeLevel`. The lemma `Claim.strip` invokes the consumer on its left-of-line branch. The target lemma itself only asks for a Dirichlet character ψ of modulus $M$, a prime natural $p$ and a complex $s$ with positive real part. Its conclusion is $1-\psi(p)p^{-s} \ne 0$. The displayed modulus binder for this lemma has no `NeZero M` argument. Preserve it. [ClaimStrip source](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CocoSgt/riemann-hypothesis-lean/0fb9869c6e6d7b54b67de803af0e0be29d37fc22/src/Development/AutomorphicArithmetic/CharacterBoxes/ClaimStrip.lean) · [consumer path](entrypoint-lean-dependency-map.md)

- **Proof to reconstruct.** Bound the norm of $\psi(p)p^{-s}$ by $p^{-\operatorname{Re}(s)} < 1$ using the character norm and the positive-prime complex-power identity. Use only the pinned official Mathlib in the clean extraction. Record the imported lemmas, exact target, source digest, toolchain, manifest, successful command log and `#print axioms` output. Record any transitive assumptions behind those imported lemmas.
- **Controls.** A principal character modulo 1 at $p=2, s=1$ gives factor $1/2$. At $p=2, s=0$ it gives zero, so weakening strict positivity to nonnegativity fails. For the principal character modulo 6, $p=2$ divides the modulus and the factor equals one. Include both character-value cases.
- **Decision.** Accept once the extracted theorem typechecks with its original quantifiers, no project imports, no placeholders or new axioms, and the source call site still matches its type. Keep the negative control with the receipt. Reject an extraction that adds primitivity, assumes nonvanishing of an L-function or uses any of the headline conclusions. A successful extraction would explain one Euler-factor step. The substantive arithmetic input to `allChi` would still require its own work.

## Finite compression as a possible common calculation

In the August paper, Lemma 3.1 bounds the positive index of a pulled-back Hermitian form by that of the original form. An off-line reflected pair contributes a block of signature (1,1) before compression. In Lemma 3.2, $P,Q$ are Hermitian matrices of the same finite size, $P$ is positive semidefinite, its rank is at most $r$, and $Q$ has at most $b$ positive eigenvalues. In these conventions the paper gives

```text
r ≥ 2 tr(P) + 4 tr(Q) − 4b − ‖P + Q‖²_HS .
```

A proposed first test uses orthogonal projections onto the two coordinate axes, with $P$ equal to the first projection and $Q$ twice the second. A second uses the off-diagonal exchange matrix with eigenvalues ±1 and restricts to the negative eigenspace. The experiments ask whether an alleged transfer tracks inertia and permits negative directions. Going from a finite compression to a statement about all zeros would additionally require control of the explicit-formula tails and analytic moments. No such November transfer has been identified here. [August v2, Lemmas 3.1–3.2, Propositions 4.1–4.3 and Theorem 5.7](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.13637v2)

## Hilbert-space simplification and short intervals: two separate control programs

[Lamzouri's 2026 independent proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02882) simplifies the **August Alpöge–Furman partial-zero proportion theorem**, substituting a Hilbert-space inequality for its finite-dimensional Hermitian matrix apparatus. [Wang's short-interval study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07918) uses Lamzouri's conjugation-invariant multiset bound and short-interval pair correlation. Neither paper proves or independently verifies the **November** GRH formalization.

**Bounded experiment:** extract an exact proposition and proof of Lamzouri's Hilbert-space inequality; check its hypotheses against Alpöge–Furman Lemma 3.2, then apply it to finite toy data and a height-window restriction. Only attempt a November transfer once a particular Lean constant and its analytic input match this functional setting. A failed match is a useful negative result, not a reason to assume a new bridge.
