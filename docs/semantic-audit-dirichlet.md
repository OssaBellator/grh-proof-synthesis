# What the five Lean targets say about Dirichlet GRH

Use classical Dirichlet GRH in the following sense. For each primitive complex Dirichlet character of positive integer conductor, all zeros of its continued L-function in $0<\Re(s)<1$ have real part $1/2$. The unique character of conductor one includes the zeta case. The three Dirichlet predicates in the pinned source give equivalent formulations of that assertion. The two zeta predicates are definitionally equal and follow from it. The proof below uses guarded nonvanishing on $\Re(s)\ge1$ as an independent analytic input. ## Declarations and domains

All three Dirichlet types quantify over `N : ℕ` with `[NeZero N]` and `χ : DirichletCharacter ℂ N`. For every such pair they quantify over `s : ℂ`, with an intervening `χ.IsPrimitive` premise in the fifth target. Write $\sigma=\Re(s)$. The two zeta types quantify over $s\in\mathbb C$ alone. Mathlib defines `DirichletCharacter.LFunction` via `ZMod.LFunction` and proves agreement with the Dirichlet series for $\sigma>1$. Away from poles this gives the continued function used in the classical formulation. Lean's type is total, so an expression at a pole has a complex value. For a principal character, the residue at $1$ is the positive product $\prod_{p\mid N}(1-p^{-1})$. That punctured-neighborhood limit concerns the meromorphic pole, independent of the assigned value at the point. The guard fails precisely for $\chi=1$ and $s=1$. It covers the holomorphic values of nonprincipal characters at $1$, including the classical Dirichlet nonvanishing theorem there. At modulus one Mathlib identifies the total functions, and even proves the assigned zeta value at $1$ is nonzero. The two zeta predicates explicitly exclude $1$ in accordance with the pole. ## Cases

In the table, “strip” refers to both Dirichlet strip predicates on their respective character families. The zeta targets apply to the zeta function itself, which occurs at modulus one.

| Input | Half-plane target | Strip targets and analytic behavior |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $N=0$ | Excluded by `[NeZero N]`. | Same exclusion. |
| $N=1$, principal | Guard excludes $s=1$. At $s=1+it$ with real $t\ne0$, the target applies. | This character is primitive. Its L-function is zeta, and the open-strip statement is the zeta zero assertion restricted to the strip. |
| $N=6$, principal | Same pole guard. | Induced from conductor one. Away from $1$, $L(s,\chi)=(1-2^{-s})(1-3^{-s})\zeta(s)$. In the open strip the extra factors are nonzero. The residue at $1$ is $1/3$. |
| Nonprincipal character mod 3, and its induction to mod 6 | Both are included at $s=1$. | At mod 3 the character takes $-1$ at $2$ and is primitive. Induction adds $1+2^{-s}$ since the factor at $3$ equals one. It leaves the open-strip zero set unchanged. |
| $\sigma<0$ or $\sigma=0$ | Outside the premise. | Outside the premise. This includes the possible extra Euler-factor zeros on $\sigma=0$. |
| $0<\sigma<1/2$ | Outside the premise at $s$. | An assumed zero here is ruled out by the strip conclusion. For the primitive inducing character, reflection sends it to a zero with real part greater than $1/2$. |
| $\sigma=1/2$ | Outside the strict premise. | A zero satisfies the conclusion. |
| $1/2<\sigma<1$ | Requires nonvanishing. The guard holds since $s\ne1$. | Equivalently excludes a zero at the same point. |
| $\sigma=1$ | Requires nonvanishing at every point passing the guard. | Outside the premise. Mathlib independently proves the guarded estimate on this line. |
| $\sigma>1$ | Requires nonvanishing, with the guard automatic. | Outside the premise. Absolute convergence of the logarithm of the Euler product gives nonvanishing. |

For the mod-3 character, the two units form a group of order two, so the nontrivial character takes $-1$ at $2$. Its conductor divides 3 and conductor 1 would give the principal character, so the conductor is 3. Extending to mod 6 keeps the value on units via reduction mod 3 and gives zero on nonunits. The level-change product uses the **source** character, so its factor at 2 is $1-(-1)2^{-s}$ even though the **induced** character vanishes there. ## Implications and analytic inputs

1. **Primitive strip implies all-character strip.** For a character mod $N$, take its inducing primitive character $\psi$ of conductor $f\mid N$. At every point of the open strip, the continued L-function is $L(s,\psi)\prod_{p\mid N}(1-\psi(p)p^{-s})$. The formula follows from the Euler products for $\sigma>1$ and continuation, with the Lean statement guarded at the principal pole. Every factor is nonzero by the [Euler-factor bound](claim-factor-extraction.md). The strip zero sets coincide. The reverse implication is restriction to primitive characters. [Conductor and primitive character](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/NumberTheory/DirichletCharacter/Basic.lean#L251-L319) · [Level change](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/DirichletContinuation.lean#L145-L162)
2. **Half-plane implies primitive strip.** A primitive strip zero with $\sigma>1/2$ directly contradicts the half-plane predicate. For $\sigma<1/2$, multiply by the finite, nonzero archimedean factor $\Gamma_{\mathbb R}(s+a)$, with $a=0$ for even and $a=1$ for odd characters. This gives a completed zero. Apply the primitive functional equation for the inverse character to obtain a completed zero of $\chi^{-1}$ at $1-s$. Both $s$ and $1-s$ have positive real part, so the gamma factor is regular and nonzero at each. The L-function of $\chi^{-1}$ therefore vanishes at $1-s$, whose real part exceeds $1/2$ and which differs from $1$. The guard holds and gives the second contradiction. Inversion preserves primitivity. This zero-transfer direction uses the functional equation by multiplying a zero, so no division by a root number is required. [Gamma and functional equation, lines 207–215 and 259–285](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/DirichletContinuation.lean#L207-L285) · [Gamma nonvanishing](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/Analysis/SpecialFunctions/Gamma/Deligne.lean#L66-L77)
3. **All-character strip implies half-plane.** For $1/2<\sigma<1$, any zero would have $\sigma=1/2$ by hypothesis. For $\sigma\ge1$ use `DirichletCharacter.LFunction_ne_zero_of_one_le_re χ hGuard hs`, which combines the separate theorem `LFunction_ne_zero_of_re_eq_one` with Euler-product nonvanishing for $\sigma>1$. The line $\sigma=1$ needs the independent boundary theorem, including nonvanishing at $s=1$ for nonprincipal real characters. Continuation and the functional equation alone do not give this estimate. [Nonvanishing, lines 383–407](https://github.com/leanprover-community/mathlib4/blob/0df444a360eaa60ab8c11dca51a86af692955474/Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/Nonvanishing.lean#L383-L407)
4. **All-character strip implies both zeta statements.** Specialize to mod 1 and use `LFunction_modOne_eq` inside the strip. Nonvanishing on $\sigma\ge1$ follows from the independent right-edge result. For $\sigma\le0$, exclude $s=0$ by $\zeta(0)=-1/2$ and exclude the negative even integers by the RH hypothesis on the candidate zero. Mathlib's total `Gammaℝ` vanishes precisely at the nonpositive even integers, the poles of its meromorphic counterpart. At the remaining points the quotient formula transfers $\zeta(s)=0$ to the completed zeta function. The completed zeta functional equation reflects the zero to $1-s$, with $\Re(1-s)\ge1$ and $1-s\ne1$ since $s\ne0$. The quotient formula then gives a zeta zero there, contradicting nonvanishing. Conversely, the zeta RH predicates give the open-strip result at modulus one, since the excluded points are outside that strip. A deduction of Dirichlet GRH from zeta RH alone would need an additional argument for the higher-conductor characters. This audit provides no such argument or logical separation result. The usual primitive meromorphic formulation calls the gamma-forced zeros trivial. For a nonprincipal primitive character and $\sigma\le0$, the reflected L-function is nonzero by the right-edge theorem, and its gamma factor is regular and nonzero. The functional equation then gives a nonzero completed value at $s$. The conductor power is nonzero since the conductor is positive. The root number is also nonzero, since a zero root number in the functional equation would force the completed function to vanish even at $2$, where both L-function and gamma factor are nonzero. The quotient by the gamma factor therefore vanishes exactly at its poles in this range, $s=-a-2k$ for $k\ge0$. For conductor one, the zeta argument above handles the exceptional point $0$ and the negative even integers. At $\sigma\ge1$ one has the independent nonvanishing theorem away from the principal pole. ## Counterexample to a broader imprimitive assertion

If the quantifier included **all** zeros of imprimitive L-functions except the negative real gamma-forced zeros, it would include extra zeros on the imaginary axis. For the principal character mod 6 take $s=2\pi i/\log 2$. Then $2^{-s}=e^{-2\pi i}=1$, while $3^{-s}$ is finite and $\zeta$ is holomorphic at $s$ because $s\ne1$. The level-change formula gives $L(s,\chi)=0$. Its real part is zero and it is nonreal, so it is neither on the critical line nor a gamma-forced zero. The same factor vanishes at $s=0$, where $\zeta(0)=-1/2$. Both points lie outside all three Dirichlet targets' premises.
